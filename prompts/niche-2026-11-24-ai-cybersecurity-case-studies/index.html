AI Cybersecurity Case Studies (YouTube Niche)

Cybersecurity case studies are the highest-RPM faceless niche in the November 2026 data — $22–$45 RPM, and a single 15-minute case study can earn $4,000–$8,000 from one 500K-view spike. True-crime structure (breach → damage → hunt) is the most watchable format on YouTube, and every breach is a ready-made story with a villain, a victim and a twist — the research does the writing for you. 📋 Copy the Prompt

Cinematic AI documentary video, [DURATION: 12-20 minutes long-form or 60-second Shorts teaser], 16:9 for long-form, 9:16 for Shorts: ONE famous cyberattack retold as a true-crime case study — [CASE: the [BREACH NAME] hack — how hackers got in, what they stole, how they were caught], [ACT 1 — THE BREACH: the quiet moment it started — one clicked link, one weak password, one unpatched server — shown as a dark control room, red alert flashing], [ACT 2 — THE DAMAGE: what was stolen and what it cost, in real numbers on screen — $[AMOUNT], [NUMBER] of accounts, [DAYS] offline], [ACT 3 — THE HUNT: how investigators traced it — the clue, the mistake the hackers made, the arrest or the escape], [NARRATION: calm documentary voice, one twist per act, present tense for tension, past tense for facts], [STYLE: cinematic, dark blue and neon green, photorealistic control rooms, evidence boards, city skylines, slow push-ins], end with 'The one setting that would have stopped it — in the description', no watermark

🚀 How to Use This Prompt

  1. Pick ONE breach per video — one company, one attack, one lesson — if the story needs two breaches, it is two videos.
  2. Structure it like true crime: breach → damage → hunt — the hunt is the payoff, so never reveal how they were caught before minute 8.
  3. End with the one setting or habit that would have stopped the attack — a copyable lesson is what gets the video saved and shared.

💡 Pro Tips

  • Hook rule: open on the damage number — '$4 billion gone in 12 minutes' — YouTube decides retention in the first 10 seconds, so the breach is the first line, not the background.
  • Title trick: 'How [COMPANY] Lost $[AMOUNT] in [TIME]' — the company name + the loss + the time is the click; the case-study format is the watch time.
  • Research lock: use only verified breach reports — a wrong number in a cybersecurity video kills the channel's trust in one comment; link your sources in the description.
