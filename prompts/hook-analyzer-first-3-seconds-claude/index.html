Hook Analyzer — Claude Prompt to Fix Why Your First 3 Seconds Fail

Viewers decide in 3 seconds — and most creators never find out why theirs fail. This Claude prompt tears apart your opening line by line, names the exact moment viewers leave, and rewrites the hook until it holds.

AI ToolClaude
CategoryText Prompts
DifficultyAdvanced

📋 Copy the Prompt

Act as a ruthless retention analyst for short-form video. Here is my video's opening: [PASTE YOUR FIRST 15 SECONDS — the exact script or a transcript of what happens on screen, second by second]. Analyse it like this: (1) SECOND-BY-SECOND — for each of the first 5 seconds, say what the viewer sees, what they feel, and whether they stay or swipe. (2) THE SWIPE MOMENT — name the exact second most viewers leave and the exact reason (boring visual, slow start, no promise, confusing, no stakes). (3) THE MISSING PROMISE — what payoff should the viewer expect by second 3 that my opening never promises? (4) REWRITES — give me 5 new hooks for the SAME video: one that opens on the ending, one that asks a question, one that makes a bold claim, one that shows the most shocking frame first, one that starts mid-action. Each hook must be under 10 words and work with the footage I already have. (5) THE FIX — tell me the ONE change to make first, the one change that would add the most watch time for the least work.

🚀 How to Use This Prompt

  1. Paste your real opening transcript into Claude — describe what is on screen second by second, not just the script.
  2. Run the analysis on your 3 worst-performing videos first — patterns across 3 videos reveal your real habit.
  3. Test the 5 rewritten hooks as 5 separate uploads of the same footage — the winner tells you your channel's hook style.
  4. Apply the ONE fix Claude names before anything else — the highest-leverage change beats a full re-edit.
  5. Re-run the analyzer on every new video's opening before you post — 2 minutes of analysis saves a dead upload.

💡 Pro Tips

  • If Claude can't find a swipe moment, your opening is probably fine — the problem is later in the video.
  • A hook that opens on the ending feels like cheating, but it is the highest-retention pattern on Shorts — test it first.
