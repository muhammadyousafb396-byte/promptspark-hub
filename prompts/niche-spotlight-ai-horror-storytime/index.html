AI Horror Storytime Parts (TikTok Niche)

Fear is the strongest retention drug — cliffhanger horror storytime turns viewers into binge-watchers, and TikTok reads the resulting completion rate as elite quality. Is niche ki videos 198.4M/167.2M/143.8M/115.9M views tak gayi hain.

📋 Copy the Prompt

AI video, [DURATION: 45-60 seconds], vertical 9:16: [SCENE: abandoned asylum corridor with flickering lights / child's bedroom at midnight with a dark silhouette in the closet / foggy forest road with a handprint on the windshield / flooded basement with a rocking chair moving alone], [STORY BEAT: the narrator reaches the twist — the figure steps closer / the silhouette's head turns / the handprint appears FROM INSIDE / the chair stops as the light swings to reveal...], [MOOD: found-footage realism / slow cinematic dread / sudden jump-scare payoff], desaturated [COLOR GRADE: blue-grey with blood-red accent / cold moonlight blue vs sickly amber], camera moves like a frightened person backing away slowly, pure dread, no text, no watermark, no visible actors' faces

🚀 How to Use This Prompt

  1. Split each story into 3-5 'Part' videos (60s each); end every part on a cliffhanger ('...and that's when the light went out. Part 4?').
  2. Script the narration with a simple 3-beat arc: normal setup, one wrong detail, implication of danger.
  3. Generate one key visual per story beat in Veo 3, then assemble in CapCut: slow zooms + heartbeat SFX + sudden silence.
  4. Record the narration in a calm, almost-bored voice — the contrast between a calm voice and scary visuals amplifies fear (the Mr. Nightmare technique).
  5. Pin Part 1 on your profile and link parts in comments so new viewers binge the whole story.

💡 Pro Tips

  • Hook = the scariest line of the story spoken in the first 2 seconds ('The rocking chair was moving. Nobody was in the room.') — never start with 'welcome back'.
  • Title formula: 'PART [N] — the [OBJECT] [DID SOMETHING IMPOSSIBLE]' — the impossible action is the curiosity gap.
  • Post at 9-11 PM when viewers are alone in the dark — horror retention spikes at night.
